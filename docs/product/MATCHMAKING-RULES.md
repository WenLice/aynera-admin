# ElAris Matchmaking and Relationship Rules

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Working rules for founder, product, safety, and advisory review  
**Applies to:** ElAris Core and ElAris Professionals

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how ElAris decides who may be considered for an introduction, how human curators make final pilot decisions, how outcomes are recorded, and how members move through Meet, Focus, Together, and Couple Space.

The first pilot will use rules plus human judgment. It will not claim that an algorithm can predict love.

---

## 2. Principles

### M-01: Compatibility is reciprocal

Member A meeting Member B’s preferences is insufficient. Both members’ hard requirements must be satisfied.

### M-02: Eligibility comes before compatibility

A profile must be approved, active, locally available, and unrestricted before matching signals are evaluated.

### M-03: Hard filters are limited

Only genuine non-negotiables should remove a potential introduction. Excessive filtering creates empty cohorts and reinforces superficial selection.

### M-04: Soft signals guide; they do not decide

Values, lifestyle, communication, and goals help curators prioritize candidates. They do not create a scientific compatibility guarantee.

### M-05: No protected or harmful inference

Do not infer caste, religion, sexuality, health, income, personality, or safety from names, photos, employers, locations, language, or behaviour proxies.

Only collect and use sensitive preferences that are lawful, necessary, explicit, and reviewed for fairness.

### M-06: Professional status is not a desirability score

Professionals membership confirms active professional identity and admission to that cohort. Job title, employer,
education, salary, and perceived prestige must not increase ranking or visibility.

### M-07: Employer prestige is not a member class

Employer identity may support workplace verification and schedule context. “Top 100,” “Tier 1,” or similar employer rankings must not create superior member classes.

### M-08: Explain reasons in human language

Member-facing reasons should be understandable and modest:

- Similar relationship pace
- Both value family time
- Compatible social rhythm
- Open to relocation
- Shared interest with compatible intent

Never show “97% compatible” or imply certainty.

### M-09: Safety overrides matching

Blocks, restrictions, credible safety concerns, and moderation decisions take priority over all compatibility signals.

### M-10: Outcomes improve operations, not member worth

Feedback should improve future introductions and safety. It must not create a hidden attractiveness or popularity score.

## 3. Matching pipeline

Every possible introduction passes through the following sequence:

1. **Account eligibility**
2. **Cohort eligibility**
3. **Reciprocal hard filters**
4. **Safety and exclusion checks**
5. **Availability and capacity**
6. **Soft compatibility assessment**
7. **Diversity/repetition review**
8. **Human curator decision**
9. **Member-facing reason creation**
10. **Outcome tracking**

Failure at stages 1–5 excludes the pair for the current evaluation. Stages 6–9 prioritize and explain eligible pairs.

---

## 4. Account eligibility

A member can enter the candidate pool only when:

- Age eligibility is confirmed
- Phone and email are verified
- Liveness/identity checks pass
- Profile review is approved
- Required consents and current policy versions are accepted
- Account is active
- Account is not suspended or restricted
- Member is not currently in Together
- Member is not already at active-connection capacity

Professionals additionally requires:

- Workplace/professional activity verified
- Professionals manual review approved

---

## 5. Cohort eligibility

### 5.1 Launch geography

The initial cohorts are:

- Delhi
- Bangalore

Members should be evaluated within a configured distance or zone policy. Exact home location must not be exposed to another member.

### 5.2 Cohort state

Each reciprocal preference segment can be:

- **Open** — enough supply for useful introductions
- **Controlled** — admissions and introductions are capped
- **Waitlisted** — insufficient reciprocal supply
- **Paused** — operational or safety reason

### 5.3 Balance over raw size

A city with 1,000 applicants can still be unusable if reciprocal preferences are severely imbalanced. Operators must evaluate:

- Gender/orientation compatibility
- Age-range overlap
- Distance overlap
- Relationship-intent overlap
- Member activity
- Response behaviour

Do not promise access from total waitlist size alone.

---

## 6. Hard filters

Hard filters remove a pair before compatibility prioritization.

### 6.1 Initial hard filters

- Reciprocal gender/orientation eligibility
- Reciprocal age range
- Active city/distance availability
- Compatible relationship intent
- Explicit member dealbreakers approved for the pilot
- Account/product cohort eligibility
- Block/restriction history between the pair
- Current relationship-state availability

### 6.2 Hard-filter rules

- Every hard filter must be explicitly supplied by the member.
- Defaults should be broad and clearly explained.
- Members can edit preferences, subject to state restrictions.
- A curator cannot silently override a hard filter.
- Product analytics must track when a filter produces no viable candidates.
- The product should encourage reconsideration, never secretly weaken a preference.

### 6.3 Potentially sensitive filters

Preferences involving religion, dietary practice, children, disability, or family plans require careful product, legal, and inclusion review before implementation.

Caste-based discovery or ranking is out of scope.

---

## 7. Soft compatibility signals

Soft signals prioritize eligible pairs and help curators reason about an introduction.

### 7.1 Relationship direction

- Serious-but-exploring versus relationship-ready
- Openness to exclusivity
- Desired pace
- Marriage interest without forcing a matrimony framing
- Children/family plans, if collected with appropriate sensitivity

### 7.2 Values and everyday life

- Family involvement
- Independence/interdependence preference
- Social rhythm
- Spending/saving outlook at a broad values level
- Health and activity preferences without medical inference
- Travel and leisure rhythm
- Homebody/adventurous balance

### 7.3 Communication

- Preferred communication frequency
- Direct versus reflective conflict style
- Text/call/in-person preference
- Need for personal space
- Openness to difficult conversations

### 7.4 Work and future plans

- Work schedule and travel
- Remote/hybrid/location constraints
- Career pace
- Relocation openness
- Five-year plans

For Professionals, use career-stage and work-life context. Do not use salary, employer prestige, job title, or educational brand as automatic rank boosts.

### 7.5 Interests

Interests are conversation starters, not the primary predictor of a relationship. One meaningful shared interest can support an introduction; identical hobby lists are unnecessary.

---

## 8. Pilot compatibility rubric

The pilot should use a transparent internal rubric rather than an opaque score.

For each eligible pair, the curator records:

### 8.1 Strong alignment

- Relationship intent and pace
- Long-term direction
- Core value compatibility
- Lifestyle feasibility

### 8.2 Useful alignment

- Communication preferences
- Work-life rhythm
- Geography and availability
- Shared interests/context

### 8.3 Tension to consider

- Relocation conflict
- Schedule conflict
- Family-expectation difference
- Social-rhythm difference
- Unclear or incomplete answer

### 8.4 Curator conclusion

- Introduce now
- Hold for later
- Needs clarification
- Do not introduce

The curator must supply two to four member-facing reasons for “Introduce now.”

---

## 9. Member-facing introduction reasons

Good reasons:

- “You both want a relationship that grows steadily rather than rushing.”
- “Both of you value close family relationships while protecting independence.”
- “Your work schedules and preferred communication rhythm appear compatible.”
- “You’re both open to building a life in Bangalore long-term.”
- “You share an interest in weekend hikes, but the stronger match is your similar relationship pace.”

Avoid:

- “You are both high-value people.”
- “They work at a top company.”
- “Your salaries match.”
- “Our AI predicts a 92% match.”
- “You look good together.”
- “Both come from good families.”

---

## 10. Introduction frequency and capacity

Initial recommendation for testing:

- Maximum 2–3 new introductions per week
- Maximum 3 active unmatched introductions at a time
- Maximum 2 active matched conversations before new introductions slow or pause

These limits are hypotheses, not permanent policy.

The pilot should test:

- Whether members feel under-served
- Whether attention and response quality improve
- Whether active-conversation capacity should vary
- Whether Professionals requires a lower frequency with stronger curation

Members in Focus or Together receive no new introductions.

---

## 11. Repetition and pool protection

- Do not re-show a passed member during the configured cooldown.
- Never reintroduce a blocked pair.
- Avoid repeatedly circulating the same highly active profiles.
- Track exposure to prevent a small subset from receiving most introductions.
- Pause inactive members from candidate supply.
- Do not punish members for privately passing.
- Curators should see prior introduction outcomes before creating a new pair.

---

## 12. Interest and matching rules

### 12.1 Expressing interest

A member should respond to a specific prompt or photo rather than sending a blank like.

### 12.2 Passing

- Pass is private.
- The passed member receives no rejection notification.
- Passing does not lower an internal member-quality score.

### 12.3 Mutual match

When both members express interest:

- Create one match atomically
- Open chat
- Notify both members
- Preserve original introduction reasons
- Make safety tools immediately available

### 12.4 Expiry

The pilot may test gentle expiry or inactivity prompts, but must not use manipulative urgency.

---

## 13. Conversation and respectful close

### 13.1 Chat

The MVP begins with text chat. Members can:

- Send messages
- View original profile/introduction context
- Report
- Block
- Close respectfully
- Start a date plan
- Request Focus when eligible

### 13.2 Respectful close

Suggested options:

- “I don’t think we’re a match .. wishing you well.”
- “I’m pausing dating for now.”
- “I’d prefer to close this conversation.”

After close:

- New messages stop
- Conversation archives privately
- The other member receives the selected neutral message
- Report remains available

---

## 14. Date and outcome feedback

### 14.1 Date plan

Members can optionally record:

- Public venue
- Date and time
- Trusted contact sharing
- Optional check-in preference

ElAris does not provide emergency response or continuous location surveillance.

### 14.2 Private feedback

After a planned date, ask:

- Did you meet?
- Would you like to meet again?
- Did you feel respected?
- Is there a safety concern?
- Optional private note

Never publish ratings or reviews on profiles.

### 14.3 Feedback use

Feedback can:

- Help a member’s future curation
- Improve cohort and introduction rules
- Trigger safety review
- Measure real outcomes

Feedback must not become a popularity leaderboard.

## 15. Focus state machine

### 15.1 Focus eligibility

Focus can be requested only when:

- Members are currently matched
- Neither account is suspended
- Neither member is in Focus/Together with someone else
- No blocking relationship exists

### 15.2 States

- `NONE`
- `REQUESTED`
- `ACTIVE`
- `ENDING`
- `EXPIRED`
- `ENDED`

### 15.3 Request

Requester selects:

- 14 days
- 30 days
- 60 days

Request shows:

- New introductions pause only after acceptance
- Either member can end Focus
- Declining does not create a public rejection event

### 15.4 Acceptance

On acceptance:

- Confirm both members are still eligible
- Activate Focus atomically
- Pause discovery for both
- Archive or quiet unrelated conversations according to the approved policy
- Set start and expiry timestamps

### 15.5 Decline

- Do not activate Focus
- Notify requester only that the request was not accepted
- Do not expose a reason unless the partner chooses to share one

### 15.6 End or expiry

At end/expiry, each member privately selects:

- Return to Meet
- Extend Focus
- Request Together
- Close the connection

Do not expose one member’s private decision until a mutual result exists.

## 16. Together state machine

### 16.1 Together eligibility

Together can be requested when:

- Members are matched
- Product policy allows direct request or requires prior Focus
- Both accounts are active
- Neither member is already in Together
- No safety restriction prevents the transition

### 16.2 States

- `NONE`
- `REQUESTED`
- `AWAITING_CONFIRMATION`
- `ACTIVE`
- `EXIT_REQUESTED`
- `COOLING_OFF`
- `ENDED`

### 16.3 Confirmation

Each member independently sees:

- Profile leaves discovery
- New likes and introductions stop
- Unrelated chats archive privately
- Couple Space opens
- Either member may leave later

Each member re-authenticates and confirms independently.

Only dual valid confirmations activate Together.

### 16.4 Activation

Activation must be atomic:

- Set both accounts to Together
- Remove both from candidate supply
- Cancel pending introductions
- Apply chat/archive policy
- Create Couple Space
- Record audit event

### 16.5 Exit

Either member can leave Together without the other’s permission.

Exit must:

- End shared access safely
- Preserve each person’s private data
- Notify the partner neutrally
- Apply an approved cooling-off period before discovery
- Keep report/block available
- Define what happens to shared notes and dates

Shared-content ownership and deletion require explicit policy before implementation.

---

## 17. Safety exclusions

The system must prevent introduction when:

- Either member blocked the other
- A safety restriction prohibits discovery
- An account is suspended or under a relevant temporary hold
- Identity/profile approval is revoked
- The pair has a prior severe safety case

Safety reviewers can:

- Remove content
- Restrict messaging
- Pause discovery
- Suspend account
- Ban account
- Escalate according to policy

Financial or professional status must never reduce scrutiny or consequences.

---

## 18. Fairness controls

The pilot should review:

- Exposure distribution
- Wait times by cohort segment
- Pass and match rates
- Reviewer decision consistency
- Professionals approval consistency
- Whether specific employers or education brands receive disproportionate exposure
- Whether manual curation reproduces caste, class, colour, disability, or other bias

Reviewer guidance must prohibit subjective labels such as:

- High value
- Good family
- Premium-looking
- Attractive enough
- Elite company
- Suitable class

Reviewer decisions must use defined eligibility, safety, completeness, and compatibility criteria.

---

## 19. Outcome taxonomy

Each introduction can end in:

- Not viewed
- Viewed
- Passed by one
- Passed by both
- Interest from one
- Mutual match
- Two-way conversation
- Conversation closed
- Date planned
- Date completed
- Focus requested
- Focus active
- Together requested
- Together active
- Safety intervention

Use outcome data to test hypotheses. Do not claim causal compatibility without sufficient evidence.

---

## 20. Pilot review cadence

### Weekly

- Cohort balance
- Introduction volume
- Response and conversation rates
- Safety cases
- Reviewer consistency
- Member support themes

### Monthly

- Matching-signal usefulness
- Professionals eligibility funnel
- Price-test results
- Date and Focus progression
- Fairness audit
- Rule changes and versioning

Every material matching-rule change should record:

- Rule version
- Reason
- Effective date
- Expected effect
- Measured result
